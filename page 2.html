History of Punjabi Weddings

Deeply rooted in the village lifestyle and rich heritage of South Asia, traditional weddings have long been vibrant community festivals that extend far beyond a single day. Historically centered around local community rhythms, seasonal harvests, and family alliances, these celebrations unite entire neighborhoods through joyful music, shared feasts, and sacred blessings. While modern Punjabi weddings have adapted to grand banquet venues and contemporary schedules, the core values of hospitality, spiritual reverence, and family honor remain beautifully preserved.

Overview of Wedding Rituals

Click each section below to explore the wedding timeline:

1. Pre-Wedding Announcements
  • Roka & Thaka: Official match announcement and exchange of gifts.
  • Sagai: Formal ring exchange ceremony.
  • Chunni Ceremony: The groom's family drapes a red scarf over the bride to welcome her.
2. Pre-Wedding Festivities
  • Maiyan / Haldi: Turmeric paste applied for a radiant glow.
  • Mehndi: Henna applied to hands and feet for good fortune.
  • Sangeet & Jaggo: Festive dance night with illuminated head lamps.
  • Choora & Kalire: Bridal bangles and hanging umbrella charms presented by uncles.
3. Wedding Day Rituals
  • Sehra Bandi: Tying the groom's ceremonial veil.
  • Baraat: Groom's grand procession with music and dancing.
  • Milni: Formal greeting between male elders of both families.
  • Anand Karaj: The sacred wedding ceremony.
4. Post-Wedding Rituals
  • Vidaai: The bride's emotional farewell to her maternal family.
  • Griha Pravesh: Welcoming the bride to her new home.
  • Reception: Celebration party with friends and extended family.
